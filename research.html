Working Papers

Economic Insecurity and Gender in Politics: How Perceptions of Economic Insecurity Shape Women's Electoral Prospects

Jhinuk Banerjee

This paper examines how economic insecurity shapes women's electoral prospects and political participation. Using measures of economic insecurity across labor markets, income, housing, and health, the study investigates how different dimensions of economic conditions influence support for women candidates and women's representation in U.S. politics.

Who Sets Fiscal Policy? Ideology, Gender and the Partisan Effects

Jhinuk Banerjee

This paper examines how the gender and political ideology of finance ministers shape fiscal policy. Using cross-country data on finance ministers and government fiscal outcomes, the study investigates whether women finance ministers and ministers with different ideological orientations are associated with systematic differences in fiscal policy and explores how political institutions condition these relationships.

Dividing Power, Building Peace: The Impact of Political Decentralization on Conflict Resolution

Jhinuk Banerjee

This paper examines the relationship between political decentralization and conflict resolution. It investigates whether distributing political authority across different levels of government can contribute to the mitigation of conflict and explores how power-sharing institutions shape political stability across democracies.
